# The three laws of the Emperor

In Aeonarch, "Emperor" does not necessarily mean the ruler of an empire: **it is a category of existence**. Emperors are humans who can persist through death and give rise to new humans without any existing population. Each player embodies one.

## I. Persistence

> The Emperor can die, but the death of their body does not necessarily mean the end of their existence.

**In the game:** if your ship is destroyed, you do not lose. Your Emperor is **reborn** in a **Lineage Cradle** (or, without Cradles, in the Human Core) after a **gestation** that grows longer if you die many times in a row, and can **incarnate in their Heirs**. Your colonies, fleets and discoveries are still yours. See [Death and lineage](/wiki/linaje).

## II. Continuity

> Humanity can reproduce autonomously. No military defeat guarantees its final disappearance.

**In the game:** your ship **generates humans by itself**, little by little, up to its capacity. That is why an Emperor with nothing can start over: they can always found a new colony. The figures are in [Humans and Law II](/wiki/humanos).

## III. Consequence

> Persistence does not grant invulnerability. Territories, resources, knowledge, armies and works can be lost forever.

**In the game:** ships destroyed in a fleet battle are gone, along with their cargo and passengers. Resources that do not fit in storage are lost. An empire too large for its volatiles production runs short of supplies. See [War](/wiki/guerra) and [Economy](/wiki/economia#upkeep).

Law III in practice: your colonies can be **besieged and conquered** ([Conquest](/wiki/conquista)); what is lost passes to someone else, with its losses.
